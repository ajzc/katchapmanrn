Soften, not erase

The fold from nose to mouth is part of how a face moves. When you smile, it deepens, and it should. Filler aims to make the fold look less deep at rest so you look less tired, while your smile still looks like yours.

If someone promises to make the fold disappear, that usually means a lot of product, and a lot of product in this area can look puffy. I'd rather leave a little crease than leave you looking filled.

Does it hurt?

Mostly pressure, with a few pinches. Some fillers already contain lidocaine, a numbing medicine. Ask for a break whenever you want one.

How long it lasts

The FDA says how long a filler lasts depends on the filler material and where it's injected, and that absorbable fillers break down over time, so the treatment may need repeating. The FDA's dermal filler page names nasolabial folds among the uses for absorbable (temporary) fillers in adults 22 and older.

Downtime

Some swelling for a few days is normal, and a bruise can happen. Most people head back to their day. Give it two weeks before photos or an event.

Safety

Before you book anywhere, read the FDA's page on dermal fillers. These are the reasons I'd ask you to wait or talk it through first:

  • Under 22: the FDA approves dermal fillers for adults 22 and older.
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding: the FDA says filler safety is unknown during both.
  • Acne, a rash, hives, or infection where we'd inject.
  • A bleeding disorder, severe allergies, past anaphylaxis, or a lidocaine allergy.
  • A history of keloids or raised scars.

Common side effects on the FDA's list are bruising, redness, swelling, pain, tenderness, and itching, and many clear up within days to weeks. The serious one is filler entering a blood vessel. The FDA calls that chance low, but it can cause tissue damage, vision problems including blindness, or stroke. If you notice unusual pain, any change in your vision, or skin near the injection site turning white, gray, or blue, get care right away.
