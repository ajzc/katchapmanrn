Neurotoxin or filler?

Injectables in Huntersville, NC split into two families that do different jobs, and a lot of first consults start with sorting out which one you need.

  • Neurotoxins (Botox, Dysport, Xeomin, Jeuveau, Letybo) relax the muscles that fold skin into lines when you move: forehead lines, the 11s, crow’s feet, bunny lines, and a lip flip. They don’t add volume.
  • Hyaluronic acid filler adds volume or structure: lips, cheeks and midface, jawline and chin, under the eyes, the temples, and the folds around the mouth. It doesn’t stop a muscle from moving.
  • Biostimulators (Sculptra, Radiesse) and PRP BioFiller work more gradually and are planned at consult.

Lines that show only when you make an expression usually point to neurotoxin. Hollows, flatness, or a soft jawline usually point to filler. Plenty of faces need a little of both, which is what facial balancing plans for.

Pain, downtime, and how long results last

Neurotoxin feels like a few quick pinches, and the injections take a few minutes. Filler takes longer, and we talk through numbing at your consult. Most people go back to work the same day. Expect the chance of a small bump, redness, or a bruise with either, and a few days of swelling after filler.

Neurotoxin settles at about two weeks, and MedlinePlus notes cosmetic Botox may be repeated every 3 to 4 months, depending on how long the effect lasts. Filler lasts longer, and how long depends on the product and the area, so I give you a range at the consult.

The FDA lists swelling and bruising among the most common dermal filler side effects. The most serious filler risk is product getting into a blood vessel. The FDA calls that chance low, but it can cause tissue damage or vision problems. For neurotoxin, the FDA prescribing information for Botox Cosmetic lists side effects such as eyelid or brow drooping and headache. Tell me at your consult if you’re pregnant or breastfeeding, or have a nerve or muscle condition.
