Two goals that look different

Some people want definition at the angle of the jaw, the corner below the ear. Product placed there makes that corner crisper and can make the face look more structured from the front.

Others want the line from the chin back to the jaw to look smooth and unbroken. That's often about filling the small dip in front of the jowl and adding a little at the chin. The result is softer and harder to name.

Many plans use a bit of both. The point is that you and I agree on the goal before any product goes in, because the placement is different.

How to describe what you want

Words like snatched or sculpted mean different things to different people. Photos help more. Bring a picture of your own face at an angle you like, or one where the jaw bothers you, and we'll work from your face instead of someone else's.

Does it hurt?

Most people feel pressure more than pain. Some fillers contain lidocaine, a numbing medicine. We can pause between sides.

How long it lasts

The FDA says how long a filler lasts depends on the filler material and where it's injected, and that absorbable fillers break down over time, so the treatment may need repeating. I won't put a number of months on your result.

Downtime

Minimal for most people. Some swelling or tenderness for a few days, and a bruise is possible. If a trip or photos are coming up, book at least two weeks ahead.

Your first visit

We agree on the goal using your photos and your face from every side. I tell you where I'd place product, roughly how much, and what it costs. Then I place it deep and deliberately. At the included two-week follow-up I check that the line matches what we agreed on and refine it if needed.

Is jawline filler safe?

Before you book anywhere, read the FDA's page on dermal fillers. These are the reasons I'd ask you to wait or talk it through first:

  • Under 22: the FDA approves dermal fillers for adults 22 and older.
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding: the FDA says filler safety is unknown during both.
  • Acne, a rash, hives, or infection where we'd inject.
  • A bleeding disorder, severe allergies, past anaphylaxis, or a lidocaine allergy.
  • A history of keloids or raised scars.

The FDA lists bruising, redness, swelling, pain, tenderness, and itching as common dermal filler side effects and says many clear up within days to weeks. The most serious risk is filler getting into a blood vessel. The FDA calls that chance low, but it can cause tissue damage, vision problems including blindness, or stroke. Get care right away for unusual pain, any change in your vision, or skin near the injection site that turns white, gray, or blue.

What filler can't do here

Filler adds structure. It won't tighten loose skin along the jaw. If cheeks are part of the balance, see cheek filler in Lake Norman, or read facial balancing in Lake Norman for a whole-face plan. The services page lists everything else.
