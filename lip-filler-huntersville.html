Does lip filler hurt?

Lips are sensitive, so I won't pretend you'll feel nothing. Numbing cream goes on first, and some fillers already contain lidocaine, a numbing medicine. Most people tell me it was far easier than they expected. If you need a pause, we pause.

How long does lip filler last?

There's no single answer, and anyone who gives you one without looking at your lips is guessing. The FDA says how long a filler lasts depends on the filler material and where it's injected, and that absorbable fillers break down over time, so the treatment may need repeating to keep the look.

HA filler can usually be dissolved with an enzyme called hyaluronidase if it ever needs to come out. That's a procedure with its own risks, but it means you're never stuck with a result you don't like.

Swelling, bruising, and downtime

Plan on a few days of swelling, and sometimes a bruise. The FDA lists swelling and bruising among the most common filler side effects and says many clear up within days to weeks. Your true shape shows at about two weeks, which is exactly when your included follow-up happens.

So don't book lips two days before a wedding, a big dinner, or photos. Give yourself at least two weeks. The FDA's dermal filler dos and don'ts also notes that swelling or redness can flare near filler after an illness, a vaccine, or dental work, so tell me if one of those is coming up.

Your first visit

We talk first. I look at your lips at rest and while you smile and talk, and we agree on a plan before I pick up a needle. Bring a photo if it helps, of your own lips from a few years ago or a shape you like. Then I build slowly and stop at natural. If you want more, we add it at your two-week follow-up instead of guessing on day one.

Some people who ask for filler really want the top lip to show a little more when they smile, without added volume. That's a lip flip, a few units of Botox, and we can sort out which one you need at the consult.

Is lip filler safe?

Lip augmentation is an FDA-approved use for hyaluronic acid fillers in adults 22 and older. The FDA's page on dermal fillers explains the risks in plain language. Based on that guidance, wait or bring it up at your consult if:

  • You're under 22. The FDA approves dermal fillers for adults 22 and older.
  • You're pregnant or breastfeeding. The FDA says filler safety is unknown during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
  • Your skin is inflamed or infected where we'd treat, including active acne, a rash, or hives.
  • You have a bleeding disorder, severe allergies or a history of anaphylaxis, or an allergy to lidocaine.
  • You tend to form keloids or raised scars.
  • There's a cold sore, swelling, or any sign of infection on or around your lips.

The FDA lists bruising, redness, swelling, pain, tenderness, and itching as common filler side effects, and says many clear up within days to weeks. The most serious risk is filler getting into a blood vessel. The FDA calls that chance low, but it can cause tissue damage, vision problems including blindness, or stroke. Get care right away for unusual pain, any change in your vision, or skin near the injection site that turns white, gray, or blue. That's the reason lip filler belongs with a licensed provider in a medical setting. You can look up my license on the North Carolina Board of Nursing site.

I can't tell you how much filler you need or how long it will last for you. Both depend on your lips, the product we choose, and how your body breaks it down.
