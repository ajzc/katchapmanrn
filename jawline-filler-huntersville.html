Why the chin matters as much as the jaw

A soft or set-back chin can make the lips and nose look bigger than they are. For some faces, a little support at the chin does more for the profile than anything else. That's why I look at your profile from every angle before we talk about the jawline, and why I'll be honest about what filler can and can't do for your anatomy.

Done well, the change reads as polished. People see a cleaner profile, and they don't see filler.

How long does it last?

Chin augmentation is one of the uses the FDA has approved for dermal fillers in adults 22 and older. FDA approval is product by product, so if you want to know exactly which filler I'd use along your jawline and what it's labeled for, ask me at your consult. The FDA also says how long a filler lasts depends on the material and where it's injected, and absorbable fillers break down over time.

I won't give you a number of months for your face. We'll see how the result is holding at your two-week follow-up.

Is jawline filler safe?

The FDA's page on dermal fillers explains the risks. Based on that guidance, wait or bring it up at your consult if:

  • You're under 22. The FDA approves dermal fillers for adults 22 and older.
  • You're pregnant or breastfeeding. The FDA says filler safety is unknown during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
  • Your skin is inflamed or infected where we'd treat, including active acne, a rash, or hives.
  • You have a bleeding disorder, severe allergies or a history of anaphylaxis, or an allergy to lidocaine.
  • You tend to form keloids or raised scars.

The FDA lists bruising, redness, swelling, pain, tenderness, and itching as common filler side effects, and says many clear up within days to weeks. The most serious risk is filler getting into a blood vessel. The FDA calls that chance low, but it can cause tissue damage, vision problems including blindness, or stroke. Get care right away for unusual pain, any change in your vision, or skin near the injection site that turns white, gray, or blue.
