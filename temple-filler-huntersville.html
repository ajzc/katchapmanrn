Why temples matter

Temples are easy to overlook. When they hollow, the top of the face can look narrower and more tired, and cheek filler alone won't fix that. Sometimes a little support at the temples does more for a tired look than anything lower down.

The label status

The temples aren't on the list of uses on the FDA's dermal filler page, so temple filler is an off-label use. I'd rather you know that before we start.

Which product?

I'll tell you which product I'd use for your temples at the consult. If it's HA filler, it can be dissolved with hyaluronidase. Sculptra and Radiesse can't be. That difference matters when we pick one.

The risk to understand

The FDA calls filler getting into a blood vessel the most concerning risk of dermal fillers. The chance is low, but it can cause tissue death, vision problems including blindness, or stroke. I wrote a full explainer on what a vascular occlusion is, with the warning signs and the treatment steps.

Get care right away for unusual pain, any change in your vision, skin near the injection that turns white, gray, or blue, or any sign of a stroke. After hours, call or text (704) 584-9785. In an emergency, call 911.

Cost

Temple filler is per syringe, quoted at consult; see the pricing page. You get a specific number before anything is booked.

Does it hurt?

Most people feel pressure and some pinches. Some fillers contain lidocaine, a numbing medicine.

Downtime

Some swelling and a possible bruise for a few days. Most people go back to their day.

How long it lasts

The FDA says how long a filler lasts depends on the filler material and where it's injected, and that absorbable fillers break down over time, so the treatment may need repeating.

Your first visit
