The FDA notes CaHA can show up on x-rays, so let your doctor or imaging team know you've had it. HA is dissolved with an enzyme called hyaluronidase.

If being able to undo it matters to you, HA is the safer choice. If we're after lift and you're comfortable with a product that stays until your body breaks it down, Radiesse can make sense.

Why reversibility matters

The FDA notes that fillers not made of materials such as hyaluronic acid can be difficult or impossible to remove. Put plainly, HA filler can be dissolved, and Radiesse can't.

Cost

Radiesse is per syringe or session, quoted at consult; see my pricing page. HA filler is quoted per syringe.

Pain and downtime

Expect pressure and pinches. Some fillers contain lidocaine, a numbing medicine. Some swelling and a possible bruise for a few days, and most people carry on afterward.

How long it lasts

The FDA's page on approved dermal fillers says the effects of calcium hydroxylapatite, the material in Radiesse, last approximately 18 months. It also notes CaHA can show up on x-rays, so let your doctor or imaging team know you've had it.

Is it safe?

Before you book anywhere, read the FDA's page on dermal fillers. These are the reasons I'd ask you to wait or talk it through first:

  • Under 22: the FDA approves dermal fillers for adults 22 and older.
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding: the FDA says filler safety is unknown during both.
  • Acne, a rash, hives, or infection where we'd inject.
  • A bleeding disorder, severe allergies, past anaphylaxis, or a lidocaine allergy.
  • A history of keloids or raised scars.

Common side effects on the FDA's list are bruising, redness, swelling, pain, tenderness, and itching, and many clear up within days to weeks. The serious one is filler entering a blood vessel. The FDA calls that chance low, but it can cause tissue damage, vision problems including blindness, or stroke. If you notice unusual pain, any change in your vision, or skin near the injection site turning white, gray, or blue, get care right away.
