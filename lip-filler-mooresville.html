Reversible is a safety net, not a plan

HA filler can usually be dissolved with an enzyme called hyaluronidase if it ever needs to come out. That's a real comfort, and it's one reason I use HA for lips. But dissolving is a procedure with its own risks, so I don't treat it as an undo button.

What that means in the chair: I place a conservative amount, stop, and let it settle. At your two-week follow-up we look at it together. If you want more, we add it then. If something needs adjusting, I do it at no charge.

Does it hurt?

Lips are sensitive, but numbing cream goes on first and some fillers already contain lidocaine, a numbing medicine. Most people feel pressure and some pinching. We can pause whenever you need to.

Swelling and downtime

Plan on a few days of swelling and the chance of a bruise. The FDA lists both among the most common filler side effects and says many clear up within days to weeks. Swelling can last a few days, and the settled result shows at your two-week follow-up. Most people go back to work the same day.

Coming from Mooresville, it's easiest to book the follow-up when you book the treatment. If there's an event on your calendar, leave at least two weeks.

How long it lasts

The FDA says how long a filler lasts depends on the filler material and where it's injected, and that absorbable fillers break down over time, so the treatment may need repeating. I give you a realistic range at your consult instead of a number on a webpage.

Your first visit

We start with what you want changed: more volume, a sharper border, evening out the top and bottom, or softening lines. I look at your lips at rest, when you talk, and when you smile, go over your health history, and agree on a plan and a price before I pick up a needle.

If what bothers you is the top lip disappearing when you smile, a lip flip may fit better than filler. For other areas, start with dermal filler in Huntersville.

Safety

The FDA's page on dermal fillers explains the risks. Wait, or bring it up at your consult, if:

  • You're under 22. The FDA approves dermal fillers for adults 22 and older.
  • You're pregnant or breastfeeding. The FDA says filler safety is unknown during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
  • Your skin is inflamed or infected where we'd treat, including active acne, a rash, or hives.
  • You have a bleeding disorder, severe allergies or a history of anaphylaxis, or an allergy to lidocaine.
  • You tend to form keloids or raised scars.
  • There's a cold sore, swelling, or any sign of infection on or around your lips.

The most serious risk is filler getting into a blood vessel. The FDA calls that chance low, but it can cause tissue damage, vision problems including blindness, or stroke. Get care right away for unusual pain, any change in your vision, or skin near the injection site that turns white, gray, or blue.
