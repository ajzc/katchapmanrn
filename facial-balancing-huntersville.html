What is facial balancing?

Most people come in for one feature. Lips, usually, or the under-eyes. Facial balancing starts from the other end. I look at how your features sit next to each other, from the front and in profile, at rest and while you talk and smile. Some injectors call it facial harmonization, and you'll also hear facial rebalancing. The idea is the same: change proportions a little in two or three places instead of a lot in one.

Here's a common example. A soft or set-back chin can make the lips and nose look bigger than they are. For some faces, a little support at the chin does more for the profile than another syringe in the lips. Whether that's true for you is something I can only tell in person, which is why every plan starts with a free consult.

Filler or Botox? Often some of each

Non-surgical facial balancing uses two different tools. Filler adds structure or volume. Botox and Dysport relax a muscle. Which one fits an area depends on whether the issue is shape or movement.

How I plan it

We start with a conversation about what bothers you, in your words. Then I tell you what I'd treat first and what I'd leave alone.

Plans often go in stages. Botox takes 3 to 14 days to settle, and filler swelling takes a few days to go down. I'd rather see your face at the two-week follow-up before adding anything. That follow-up is included, and if something needs adjusting, I adjust it at no charge.

One thing worth knowing: the FDA notes that using fillers together with neuromodulators like Botox hasn't been evaluated in a controlled clinical study. If your plan uses both, ask me about it at your consult.

Does it hurt, and what's the downtime?

Botox feels like a quick pinch. Some fillers already contain lidocaine, a numbing medicine, and most people feel pressure more than pain. You can ask me to stop at any point.

Botox has no real downtime. Filler can leave swelling for a few days, and bruising is possible. The FDA says many of those side effects clear up within days to weeks. If you have a big event, give yourself at least two weeks.

How long does facial balancing last?

It depends on which part of the plan you mean. The FDA says how long a filler lasts depends on the filler material and the area where it's injected, and that some fillers are absorbed over time, so the treatment may need repeating.

Botox wears off too. MedlinePlus says cosmetic Botox injections for frown lines, forehead lines, and crow's feet may be repeated every 3 to 4 months, depending on how long the effect lasts. I won't promise a number for your face. We can talk about touch-up timing at your two-week follow-up.

If you change your mind

The filler I use is Juvéderm, a hyaluronic acid (HA) filler. Different HA fillers do different jobs, and I pick the right one for you at your consult. Sculptra and Radiesse are not HA and can't be dissolved the same way. HA filler can usually be dissolved with an enzyme called hyaluronidase if it needs to come out. That's a procedure with its own risks, and the FDA notes that non-HA and permanent fillers can be difficult or impossible to remove. Botox can't be dissolved. It wears off on its own, which is one reason I dose on the light side and add at two weeks if you want more.

When to wait, and who it's not for

Based on the FDA's dermal filler guidance, plan to wait, or bring it up at your consult before anything is injected, if:

  • You're under 22. The FDA approves dermal fillers for adults 22 and older.
  • You're pregnant or breastfeeding. The FDA says filler safety is unknown during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
  • Your skin is inflamed or infected where we'd treat, including active acne, a rash, or hives.
  • You have a bleeding disorder, severe allergies or a history of anaphylaxis, or an allergy to lidocaine.
  • You tend to form keloids or raised scars.
  • You have a big event in the next two weeks. Give swelling and Botox time to settle.

If you want to look like a different person, I'm the wrong injector. Facial rebalancing the way I do it means small changes that still look like you.

Risks, and what I can't promise

The FDA lists bruising, redness, swelling, pain, tenderness, and itching as common filler side effects, and says many clear up within days to weeks. The most serious risk is filler getting into a blood vessel. The FDA calls that chance low, but it can cause tissue damage, vision problems including blindness, or stroke. Get care right away for unusual pain, any change in your vision, or skin near the injection site that turns white, gray, or blue. For Botox, MedlinePlus explains the rare risk of the drug spreading beyond where it was injected. You can look up my license on the North Carolina Board of Nursing site.
