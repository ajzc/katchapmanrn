Does it hurt?

Most people feel pressure more than pain. Some fillers already contain lidocaine, a numbing medicine, which takes the edge off as I go. You can ask me to stop at any point.

How long does cheek filler last?

Cheek augmentation is one of the uses the FDA has approved for dermal fillers in adults 22 and older. The FDA also says how long a filler lasts depends on the material and where it's injected, and that absorbable fillers break down over time, so the treatment may need repeating.

I won't promise you a number of months. We'll look at how your result is holding at your two-week follow-up and talk about what touch-ups might look like down the road.

Downtime

The visit typically takes under an hour. Bruising is possible but usually minor, and some swelling for a few days is normal. Most people go back to their day afterward. If you have an event, book at least two weeks ahead so you're looking at the settled result.

Placement is structural and conservative, working with your bone structure. Your included two-week follow-up is where the result gets checked and refined.

Lines from the nose to the mouth sometimes soften when support comes back to the midface above them. Whether that's true for you is something I can only tell in person.

Is cheek filler safe?

The FDA's page on dermal fillers explains the risks. Based on that guidance, wait or bring it up at your consult if:

  • You're under 22. The FDA approves dermal fillers for adults 22 and older.
  • You're pregnant or breastfeeding. The FDA says filler safety is unknown during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
  • Your skin is inflamed or infected where we'd treat, including active acne, a rash, or hives.
  • You have a bleeding disorder, severe allergies or a history of anaphylaxis, or an allergy to lidocaine.
  • You tend to form keloids or raised scars.

The FDA lists bruising, redness, swelling, pain, tenderness, and itching as common filler side effects, and says many clear up within days to weeks. The most serious risk is filler getting into a blood vessel. The FDA calls that chance low, but it can cause tissue damage, vision problems including blindness, or stroke. Get care right away for unusual pain, any change in your vision, or skin near the injection site that turns white, gray, or blue.
